This page documents how PICurv initializes Eulerian velocity fields and particles at startup. It covers both user-facing YAML inputs and the C implementation path that applies them.

1. Where Initialization Happens

Startup sequence:

  1. picurv_cli/core.py resolves the source.
  2. A file or generator term is staged in the filename layout expected by ReadFieldData.
  3. InitializeEulerianState chooses fresh solve, restart load, or analytical initialization.
  4. On a fresh solve, PopulateInitialUcont populates Ucont; existing finalization then applies boundary values and derives the remaining velocity state.
  5. If particles are enabled, InitializeParticleSwarm runs independently after Eulerian setup.

2. Eulerian Field Initialization (<tt>properties.initial_conditions</tt>)

An initial condition is one source and optional constraints, the same definition a prescribed_velocity inlet takes. Its forms:

source Field
{components, constants, expression}, or a name under sources: evaluated by the solver (Source: a velocity written as expressions)
{generator, params} written by a Python generator, staged (3. Staged Field Entries)
{type: file, field, path} read from a velocity file, staged
{sum: [...]} expressions added to at most one staged field (Source: a velocity written as expressions)

The same language gives particle-carried fields their initial values: one expression per particle, which also reads pid (1.1 Configured Particle Values (fields)).

A velocity file:

properties:
initial_conditions:
source: {type: file, field: Ucat, path: initial_conditions/velocity.dat}

The input must be one PETSc binary .dat vector readable by ReadFieldData. Ucat inputs are converted by Cart2Contra; Ucont inputs are used directly. The first implementation supports one block only because it intentionally reuses the existing single-field ReadFieldData path.

The payload is physical: Ucat in velocity units, Ucont in volume flux per face. It is divided by velocity_ref (or velocity_ref length_ref^2) as it is staged. A field saved by an earlier PICurv run is in that run's solver units, so it declares the scales it was written in, and staging rescales it to this case's:

source:
type: file
field: Ucat
path: inputs/initial_conditions/velocity.dat
source_case: runs/<earlier_run>/config/case.yml # or: velocity_scale: 1.5

velocity_scale needs length_scale too for a Ucont payload, and source_case and the explicit scales are mutually exclusive.

Source: a velocity written as expressions

An expression source is given inline or by name from the case's top-level sources::

sources:
duct_flow:
components: curvilinear # cartesian (default) | curvilinear
constants: {U: 1.0}
expression: {u_zeta: "36*U*xi*(1-xi)*eta*(1-eta)"}
properties:
initial_conditions:
source: duct_flow
constraints: {scale: 1.0} # optional

The runtime evaluates it at every cell centre, in physical units, over x y z, t (the start time), the arc-length fractions xi eta zeta and the line lengths L_xi L_eta L_zeta, and writes Ucat; Cart2Contra then derives Ucont. A curvilinear source sets the fluxes instead, as below. The fractions follow distance along each grid line, so a profile written in them is the physical profile on a stretched grid, unlike the index fractions a grid-line count would give. The normal frame is a face's frame and is refused here.

source may instead be a sum of terms: sources (names or mappings) in one frame, which are added, and at most one staged field: a {generator, params} term or a {type: file, ...} term. The field is staged as it would be on its own, and the runtime adds the evaluated source to it; under a staged Ucont the expressions must be curvilinear. A mean profile plus the turbulent fluctuations of a wall seed:

properties:
initial_conditions:
source:
sum:
- inflow # a named source: the mean
- generator: spectral_velocity # fluctuations only; wall_axes [j] is a channel
params: {seed: 12345, wall_axes: [j], streamwise_axis: k,
normalization: {type: component_rms, target: 0.1}, initial_spectra: [...]}
constraints: {bulk_velocity: 1.0, project: true}

constraints apply to the whole field, in order. scale multiplies it. bulk_velocity [m/s], a number or an expression in t, means what it means on an inlet (p44_cap_prescribed_velocity): the source's expressions are rescaled, and anything staged is left as it is, so that the mean of the whole equals it. Here the mean is over the cells with volume weights, along the source's own direction at each cell; an inlet's is over its face with area weights, along the normal. It needs an expression to rescale. project, which defaults to true for a sum, written in place or named under sources:, and false otherwise, makes it divergence-free before the first step with the time step's own pressure projection: one Poisson solve for the initial Ucont, whose boundary fluxes are already set by the boundary conditions, and the correction of the interior faces. The potential is discarded, so pressure keeps its initial value, and the solve's convergence record is overwritten by the first step's. Without project, the first step's projection makes the field divergence-free instead, after one momentum step on the field as given. Projecting evens each plane's flux to the same value without changing bulk_velocity's volume mean on a periodic axis; between an inlet and an outlet, conservation sets each plane's flux to the inlet's, so a bulk_velocity other than the inlet's does not survive it. A source's pressure expression [Pa] sets the initial P at the cell centres, and is otherwise zero.

A velocity or pressure expression may draw uniform(), in [0, 1), and normal(), from N(0, 1), to perturb a field with white noise:

source_random_seed: 7 # top level; optional, default 12345
properties:
initial_conditions:
source: {expression: {u: "0.1*normal()", v: "0.1*normal()", w: "1 + 0.1*normal()"}}
constraints: {project: true}

Each cell draws once per component, and sets the faces it owns with that draw. Each component has its own default stream; normal(k) with a non-negative integer literal k names a stream that components can share. Calling normal() twice in one expression gives the same number twice, so independent terms need different streams. A draw is a pure function of the seed, the block, the cell's global index and the stream, so a field is the same at any rank layout, and source_random_seed varies the flow without changing the draws particles make under models.physics.particles.random_seed.

Two consequences of drawing per cell. Each face has the amplitude written, while a cell centre averages two faces: 0.1*normal() gave a face rms of 0.100 and a cell-centre rms of 0.071, which is 1/sqrt(2) as much, on a 24^3 periodic box. Noise is not divergence-free, so a projection removes about a third of its energy: project: true kept 0.664 of the face energy there, against 2/3 for isotropic white noise. Without project, the first step's projection removes that third instead. The field has no spatial correlation, and a different grid draws a different field, so it seeds instability; for a prescribed spectrum use a generator (spectral_velocity). bulk_velocity reads the same draws, as the mean along each cell's own direction, so noise across the mean adds to the speed it scales; an analytical field refuses draws, since it is an exact answer.

Every component reaches the velocity through the faces, evaluated at each face centre. A Cartesian velocity is dotted with the face metric. A curvilinear component is the speed through the grid face normal to its axis, on every target: the flux is that component times the face area. A contravariant component is the volume flux through that face itself [m^3/s], so it writes Ucont directly:

source: {components: contravariant, expression: {u_zeta: "0.01*(1 + x*y)"}}

An initial condition takes the contravariant frame; an inlet and an analytical field do not, and bulk_velocity reads a velocity, so it does not apply to one. A uniform u_zeta is therefore discretely divergence-free on a curved grid whose zeta faces keep their area along the line, and needs no projection.

Closed-form profiles: series and special functions

A source's expressions, like particle values, may use a series and four special functions, which the solver evaluates:

Construct Meaning
sum(k, lo, hi, body) body summed for the integers k = lo, ..., hi, with k in scope only in body. The bounds are integer numbers (a constant inlines to one); at most 1000000 terms; series nest up to four deep, each with its own index
besselj(n, x), bessely(n, x) Bessel functions of the first and second kind, of integer order n (another order gives a value that is not finite, which stops the run)

| ber(x), bei(x) | Kelvin functions of order zero, ber(x) + i bei(x) = J0(x e^{3 pi i/4}), from their power series below |x| = 30, accurate to 1e-13 relative there, and their asymptotic expansion above it |

picurv validates these before launch and the solver evaluates them. Two exact laminar profiles, in the form tests/test_expression_language.py and make unit-particles check:

sources:
duct_poiseuille:
components: normal
constants: {U: 1.0, beta: 1.0}
expression: >-
U*sum(k, 0, 99, (-1)**k/(2*k+1)**3
*(1 - (exp(abs((2*k+1)*pi*beta*(eta - 0.5)) - (2*k+1)*pi*beta/2)
+ exp(-abs((2*k+1)*pi*beta*(eta - 0.5)) - (2*k+1)*pi*beta/2))/(1 + exp(-(2*k+1)*pi*beta)))
*cos((2*k+1)*pi*(xi - 0.5)))
/ sum(k, 0, 99, 2/(pi*(2*k+1)**4)*(1 - 2*tanh((2*k+1)*pi*beta/2)/((2*k+1)*pi*beta)))
womersley:
components: normal
constants: {U: 1.0, K: 1.0, omega: 6.283185307179586, alpha: 5.0, R: 0.5}
expression: >-
2*U*(1 - (x**2 + y**2)/R**2)
+ (K/omega)*((ber(alpha*sqrt(x**2 + y**2)/R)*bei(alpha) - bei(alpha*sqrt(x**2 + y**2)/R)*ber(alpha))
/(ber(alpha)**2 + bei(alpha)**2)*cos(omega*t)
+ (1 - (ber(alpha*sqrt(x**2 + y**2)/R)*ber(alpha) + bei(alpha*sqrt(x**2 + y**2)/R)*bei(alpha))
/(ber(alpha)**2 + bei(alpha)**2))*sin(omega*t))

duct_poiseuille is the fully developed flow in a rectangular duct whose section has width along xi and height along eta, beta = height / width, at bulk velocity U: the cosh-cosine series of the exact solution over its own area mean, both to 100 odd terms, with cosh(a)/cosh(b) written so that it cannot overflow. On a square section it matches the double sine series of the same solution to 1e-5 of U; written in the arc-length fractions, it is the physical profile on a stretched grid. womersley is the flow in a pipe of radius R along z, with its axis through x = y = 0: a Poiseuille mean of bulk U plus the exact oscillatory part driven by -(1/rho) dp/dz = K cos(omega t) [m/s^2], with Womersley number alpha = R sqrt(omega/nu). It matches the complex solution Re{(K/(i omega))(1 - J0(i^{3/2} alpha s)/J0(i^{3/2} alpha)) e^{i omega t}} to 1e-12, and at small alpha it tends to the quasi-steady Poiseuille profile. A sweep does not place a pipe there by default; on an inlet, r replaces sqrt(x**2 + y**2) and the profile holds wherever the pipe lies, with its centre on the face's. Either serves an inlet as written and an initial condition with components: curvilinear and the expression under u_zeta, on a grid whose zeta lines run along the flow.

File, Or Import?

Every Python provider ends in a .dat vector; what differs is who guarantees it fits the case.

type: file generator: resampled_velocity
You supply a vector already in PICurv's layout for this grid a field from anywhere: another code, resolution, or layout
PICurv does reads it verbatim resamples or interpolates it onto this grid, projects it, writes the vector
Typical source a field PICurv itself produced a published DNS, an experiment, another code's snapshot

A type: file vector sized for another grid is refused when it is staged, with a pointer to p33_cap_gen_resampled_velocity.

Your own generator:

properties:
initial_conditions:
source:
generator: ic_gen
params:
field: Ucat
script: tools/custom_ic.py
config_file: config/initial_conditions/custom.cfg

picurv chooses where the payload goes: it is always written to <run.inputs>/initial_condition/initial_condition.generated.dat. The retired destination keys output_file, summary_json, and spectrum_csv are refused with an error rather than ignored.

The launcher runs the script, resolved against the case, as:

python <script> -c <config_file> --field Ucat|Ucont --output <run.inputs>/initial_condition/initial_condition.generated.dat --grid <run.inputs>/grid/grid.run --length-ref <L> --velocity-ref <U> --petsc-index-bytes <4|8>

It receives the non-dimensional staged grid and the case's reference scales, and writes solver units. grid.mode: file and grid.mode: grid_gen provide that grid directly; for single-block grid.mode: programmatic_c, the launcher materializes a nondimensional <run.inputs>/grid/grid.run bridge from scalar programmatic_settings first. picurv run --solve materializes the result after grid preparation, and picurv precompute --case ... materializes and stages the same artifact without running the solver.

Repository spectral provider, for a triply periodic box:

properties:
initial_conditions:
source:
generator: spectral_velocity
params:
field: Ucat
seed: 12345
spectrum: {type: k4_exponential, k0: 4.0, k_cut: 20.0}
projection: {type: solenoidal, operator: picurv_discrete}
normalization: {type: component_rms, target: 1.0}
remove_mean: true

It draws a seeded random field, shapes it to the spectrum envelope (k4_exponential is E(k) ~ k^4 exp(-2 (k/k0)^2), nothing above k_cut), projects it to be divergence-free under the chosen operator, and scales it to the requested component RMS. random.mean and normalization.target are velocities and k0, k_cut wavenumbers, all physical, and are converted before the provider runs on the staged non-dimensional grid. It requires every face geometric-periodic and a fresh 3D run, and writes <run.analysis>/metrics/initial_condition_summary.json and the staged spectrum beside the field. examples/decaying_isotropic_turbulence configures it; its README covers the keys. With operator: picurv_discrete the solver's own step-0 divergence is at round-off; initial-conditions-2026-09-21 measured 3.1e-14 against a flux scale of 4.3, component RMS 1.000000, the shell spectrum within 4.7% (energy-weighted) of the envelope, and the same seed reproducing the same field.

3. Staged Field Entries

The file term and the expression config ic_gen once evaluated were checked against the field they claim to produce by initial-conditions-2026-09-21: on a programmatic duct the staged interior face fluxes equal their definitions to 3.5e-18 or exactly, and a file term reproduces the generated run it was taken from bitwise. Step-0 Ucat next to walls and inlets reflects the boundary faces, because it is reconstructed from the fluxes after boundary conditions apply.

Value Maps to
ucatucat
ucontucont

Ucat

Identity. field: Ucat on a file or ic_gen term -> the Cartesian velocity field is what is staged (internal code 0).

What it does. Stages the Cartesian velocity, from which the contravariant fluxes are then derived.

When to choose it. The normal choice, and the one that matches how the field modes are expressed - a uniform or parabolic profile is naturally stated in Cartesian components.

Parameters it owns. None; it selects which field the term holds.

Interactions. Ucat is the derived representation, so initializing it requires a conversion to the evolved Ucont fluxes before the first step. See Grid, Cell, and Variable Architecture Guide.

Diagnostics. Step-0 output shows the initialized field.

Evidence. Production exercised - examples/flat_channel. Analytically verified - initial-conditions-2026-09-21: Ucat expressions staged the fluxes their Cartesian values define.

Limitations. On a strongly curvilinear grid the conversion from a Cartesian profile to fluxes is not exact in the sense of preserving the intended profile shape.

Ucont

Identity. field: Ucont on a file or ic_gen term -> the contravariant flux field is what is staged (internal code 1).

What it does. Stages the evolved flux variable directly, skipping the Cartesian-to-flux conversion.

When to choose it. When you want exact control of the initial fluxes - for instance setting a precise volumetric flux - rather than of the Cartesian velocity.

Parameters it owns. None.

Interactions. Ucont is what the solver actually evolves, so this path avoids one conversion. The field modes are still expressed in the same components, which is what makes this the less intuitive of the two.

Diagnostics. Step-0 output; check the derived Ucat looks as intended.

Evidence. Analytically verified - initial-conditions-2026-09-21: ic_gen Ucont expressions stage exactly, and a file source of Ucont reproduces the generated run bitwise. No shipped example selects it.

Limitations. Easier to get wrong than Ucat, because the values are fluxes rather than velocities: a face flux carries the face area, so a velocity typed where a flux is expected is off by that area. Only uniform Cartesian grids were checked.

4. C Runtime Entry Point

PopulateInitialUcont is the fresh-solve entry point. It reads a staged field when -ic_dir names one, through ReadFieldData; when that field is Ucat, Cart2Contra converts it to Ucont. It then evaluates the source's expressions, through the same PicurvVelocitySource an inlet uses. After that point, the existing finalization path treats every initial condition identically.

5. Authority and Restart Branches

In InitializeEulerianState:

  • eulerian_field_source=solve and StartStep == 0 consumes initial_conditions.
  • eulerian_field_source=solve and StartStep > 0 consumes the restart path.
  • eulerian_field_source=load consumes the existing load path.
  • eulerian_field_source=analytical consumes the analytical initializer.

Thus eulerian_field_source supersedes initial_conditions; the launcher does not materialize a configured file or ic_gen artifact when another source has authority.

Operational note:

  • StartStep identifies the saved restart state being loaded, not the first new step to compute.
  • If a run completed through step N, restart with start_step: N; the first newly advanced step will be N+1.

7. Practical Checks

After startup, confirm:

  • the banner names the initial condition as the Eulerian source, and any staged file,
  • no warnings about missing inlet face (for inlet-driven setups),
  • first output step has non-empty Ucat/Ucont fields.

Common pitfalls:

  • forgetting that initialization sets the interior only; boundary handlers then overwrite face values,
  • providing a PETSc vector whose size does not match the target DM,
  • comparing a velocity directly to Ucont without accounting for metric-face scaling.

Spectral velocity: periodic boxes, channels and ducts

Value Maps to

resampled_velocityresampled_velocity
spectral_velocityspectral_velocity

spectral_velocity takes wall_axes: none for a triply periodic box, one for a channel, two for a duct. In a box it writes a seeded random field shaped by a spectrum (the parameters in p33_cap_gen_spectral_velocity). With walls, the remaining axes are periodic, with driving allowed only on streamwise_axis, or the streamwise axis runs from an inlet on its negative face to an outlet on its positive face. The seed's modes are periodic along that axis either way; between an inlet and an outlet, project the sum (p33_source). Both use Ucat on one Cartesian block. Uniform spacing is required along periodic axes; wall-normal axes may stretch. grid_gen provides an inspectable staged grid.

source:
sum:
- {components: cartesian, expression: {w: "6*eta*(1-eta)"}}
- generator: spectral_velocity
params:
field: Ucat
seed: 12345
wall_axes: [j]
streamwise_axis: k
normalization: {type: component_rms, target: 0.1}
wall_modes: 3
spectrum: {type: k4_exponential, k0: 4.0, k_cut: 8.0}
initial_spectra:
- {task: plane_spectrum, axes: [i, k], fixed_indices: {j: 32}, subtract_mean: sample}
constraints: {bulk_velocity: 1.0, project: false}

With walls the generator writes the fluctuation alone; the mean is a source, normalized by bulk_velocity. Parameters are physical: normalization.target is a velocity and the spectrum wavenumbers are per length, converted before the provider runs. The target is ‘sqrt(volume_mean(u’^2+v'^2+w'^2)/3), not the RMS of each component independently, the same measure a periodic box'scomponent_rmsuses; with walls it is required, defaulting to 0.1, because the curl of the vector potential has no amplitude of its own. The spectral envelope shapes a seeded vector potential along periodic directions; wall basis functions aresin(n*pi*t)*sin(pi*t), evaluated at physical cell centers: the least envelope that keeps the potential and its wall-normal derivative zero at the wall, so the seed has the physical near-wall behaviour, wall-parallel u' ~ yand wall-normalv' ~ y^2`. A curl using the separable face-average divergence operators produces a perturbation in the discrete divergence nullspace, including zero wall-face flux. Periodic modes at or above one third of the cell count are removed. Wall mode count plus one (the highest sine index added by the envelope) must fit below half the wall-axis cell count; stretched meshes still need a physical resolution check. The envelope is not a prescribed final isotropic velocity spectrum: the curl, wall functions, and stretching change it.

Perturbation net flux is zero, so the bulk velocity is the mean's. Match the driven boundary's target flux to bulk velocity times cross-section. A curl of periodic modes plus a streamwise-uniform mean is already divergence-free, so a periodic case need not project. Wall dummy values are odd reflections, periodic dummies wrap. Runtime retains ownership of boundary enforcement and Cartesian/contravariant reconstruction.

initial_spectra is a required nonempty list using the plane/line task syntax from 8. spectra. Initial measurements cannot use a statistics-window mean. Staging measures the generated Ucat, before runtime reconstruction. Compare against step-zero checkpoint spectra to distinguish initialization reconstruction from subsequent physical evolution. The summary and spectra use the canonical metrics and spectra directories, including through precomputed asset publication and reuse. There is no per-timestep injection, and transition/sustained turbulence is not guaranteed.

ic_gen

Identity. source: {generator: ic_gen} dispatches through GENERATED_IC_PROVIDERS into generators/ic.gen, then the staged-field runtime path.

What it does. Runs your own script, which writes a Ucat or Ucont vector for the staged grid.

When to choose it. A field that needs a program. A field with a formula is an expression source (Source: a velocity written as expressions), whose language this page and the solver share.

Parameters it owns. Requires script and config_file; field selects Ucat/Ucont; cli_args are appended.

Interactions. The script reads the staged PICGRID and writes the PETSc vector; a missing input or a vector sized for another grid fails before the solver launches.

Diagnostics. The script's output is reported when it fails.

Evidence. Unit verified - make test-python covers the script contract and the CLI route. initial-conditions-2026-09-21 measured the expression config this generator once evaluated, whose Ucat and Ucont fields the source frames reproduce: exactly on a flat channel for a test field with no variation along each face's normal, and to 3e-15 for Ucont on a curved one. A Ucat expression differs there by 5e-7 in flux, because a source evaluates at the face.

Limitations. Nothing is implied about a field a script writes.

spectral_velocity

Identity. source: {generator: spectral_velocity} dispatches through GENERATED_IC_PROVIDERS into generators/ic.gen, then the staged-field runtime path. params.wall_axes chooses the geometry: omitted or [] a triply periodic box, one axis a channel, two a duct.

What it does. In a box, a seeded random field shaped by a spectrum, optionally projected solenoidal and rescaled. With walls, a discrete-curl spectral perturbation vanishing at each wall pair, with no mean (Spectral velocity: periodic boxes, channels and ducts).

When to choose it. Decaying isotropic turbulence in a box; a channel or duct start, as the fluctuation term of a sum whose mean is a source.

Parameters it owns. Every geometry: field, seed, spectrum, normalization, wall_axes. A box adds random, projection and remove_mean, and normalization may be {type: none}. With walls: streamwise_axis, wall_modes and a required nonempty initial_spectra, and normalization must be component_rms. Parameters that belong to the other geometry are refused.

Interactions. One 3D Cartesian block and Ucat staging. A box needs six geometric-periodic faces and uniform spacing; with walls the faces follow Spectral velocity: periodic boxes, channels and ducts, the wall-normal axes may stretch, and immersed geometry is refused. Restart remains authoritative.

Diagnostics. A box's summary reports the selected operator's divergence and realized energy, and shell spectra measure the generated field. With walls the summary reports perturbation RMS, component means and discrete divergence, and the selected plane or line spectra measure the seed.

Evidence. Unit verified - make test-python. Production exercised in examples/decaying_isotropic_turbulence (box), examples/turbulent_channel (channel) and examples/periodic_test/driven_duct (duct). Analytically verified:

  • initial-conditions-2026-09-21 (box): fluctuation RMS 1.000000 and kinetic energy 1.500000 as requested, the solver's own step-0 divergence 3.1e-14 against a flux scale of 4.3 under operator: picurv_discrete, the shell spectrum within 4.7% (energy-weighted) of the k4_exponential envelope with nothing above k_cut, and identical seeds reproducing identical fields.
  • wall-spectral-ic-2026-09-24 (channel and duct): on stretched 32 x 32 x 64 grids, when the generator owned a flux-normalized mean that the source sum now reproduces to 4e-13, the perturbation RMS is exactly the requested 0.1 with zero component means, the dummy layers are exact odd reflections so the wall faces carry zero velocity, the runtime's step-0 flux field has a cell divergence of at most 1.4e-17 against flux scales of 2.7e-2 to 4.1e-2, and the wall-parallel perturbation grows linearly from each wall (first-to-second cell RMS ratios 2.42 to 2.44, the discrete curl's value for a potential ~ y^2).

These measurements were made under the generator's earlier names, before the three were one selector; the generated fields are byte-identical to theirs.

Limitations. A startup field, not developed turbulence. In a box, a continuum solenoidal field need not be discretely solenoidal - choose operator: picurv_discrete when the solver's own divergence must vanish - and runtime reconstruction changes the energy from the staged value, which the summary predicts separately. With walls the spectrum shapes the vector potential rather than the velocity; circular pipes and immersed boundaries are outside it; the measurements were on reduced grids on one rank, and cover a periodic streamwise axis only - the inlet-outlet axis is exercised by tests/test_velocity_sources.py and a one-period demonstration run.

resampled_velocity

Identity. source: {generator: resampled_velocity} dispatches through GENERATED_IC_PROVIDERS into generate_resampled_velocity() in generators/ic.gen, then the file IC runtime path; the Fourier resampling is fourier_resample in picurv_cli/periodic_spectral.py.

What it does. Brings an external velocity field onto this case's grid, by one of two paths.

  • interpolation: fourier, the default on a triply periodic uniform box, re-evaluates the source at the target points from its Fourier series - truncating when the grid is coarser, zero-padding when it is finer - optionally filters it, projects it to be divergence-free under PICurv's discrete operator, and writes solver units. With field: Ucont (the default) it samples face centres and stages volume fluxes, the representation PICurv evolves; with field: Ucat it stages cell-centre velocities like the synthetic providers.
  • interpolation: linear (the default on any other grid) or cubic reads samples on rectilinear source axes and interpolates them to the case's cell centres, each the mean of its eight nodes as the runtime's own. The grid may be curvilinear and its faces any boundary type. The velocity is staged as Cartesian Ucat; the runtime turns it into face fluxes with the grid's metrics, applies the boundary conditions, and with projection: picurv_discrete makes it divergence-free with the case's own pressure solve (the project constraint). This path is experimental.

When to choose it. When the starting field exists already: a published DNS or experiment field, a snapshot from another code, or a field at another resolution. spectral_velocity synthesizes a field from a spectrum instead, and suits decay-law and parametric studies where the initial spectrum is the variable. mode: file reads a vector already laid out for this grid, verbatim; see File, Or Import?.

Parameters it owns. Required: source_file (a workspace input, normally a reference-field import) and format (raw, npy, npz, or hdf5). The source's sample positions come from one of two descriptions. A uniform box: box_length, its three lengths in physical units, and optionally sample_offset (fraction of the source spacing at which samples sit, 0 for nodes on the box origin and 0.5 for cell centres; default 0) and, off the Fourier path, box_origin, its lower corner (default 0). stability_mode_velocity

Identity. source: {generator: stability_mode_velocity} dispatches through GENERATED_IC_PROVIDERS into generators/ic.gen, then the staged-field runtime path.

What it does. A parallel base flow plus Rayleigh eigenmodes at chosen streamwise harmonics and optional divergence-free noise (Linear-stability mode seeds).

When to choose it. Temporal mixing layers, jets and wakes forced at their natural instability, and linear growth-rate checks of the solver.

Parameters it owns. streamwise_axis, cross_axis, base_profile, modes (harmonic, amplitude, phase), velocity_difference, eigen_points, noise, and the optional initial_spectra over the periodic axes.

Interactions. Single-block axis-aligned Cartesian grid; geometric periodic streamwise and normal axes; non-periodic cross-stream faces. In 2D mode the normal axis must be i and noise.planar true. Refuses a harmonic at or above a third of the streamwise cells and a profile with no unstable mode at a requested harmonic.

Diagnostics. The initial-condition summary reports, per mode, the wavenumber, complex phase speed, growth rate (solver and physical units), the streamwise peak the case grid holds (gridded_peak_over_dU) and the streamwise amplitude left at the cross-stream ends; a growth-rate scan over the resolvable harmonics and the fastest-growing one, for sizing the box; and the discrete divergence.
